# Tech's Record-Revenue Layoffs Are Not an AI Story. They're a Story About Who Controls the Narrative.

**2026-06-03**

GitLab confirmed today that it's cutting 350 people — 14% of its workforce — and exiting 22 countries, reducing its geographic footprint by 37%. CEO Bill Staples called it "GitLab Act 2." He says the company is "rewiring internal processes with AI agents" and positioning itself for the "agentic era."

What he didn't say: GitLab posted $955 million in revenue last fiscal year, up 26% year-over-year. Free cash flow was $220 million, up more than 80%. The company authorized a $400 million share buyback. None of that is a company in distress.

So why are 350 people out of a job?

---

## The script is the same everywhere

GitLab is the latest entry in a pattern that's become almost formulaic in 2026:

- **Cloudflare**, May 7: cuts 1,100 employees (20% of staff), citing AI agents making roles obsolete. Same announcement: Q1 revenue $639.8 million, up 34% YoY, best quarter in company history.
- **Cisco**, May 14: nearly 4,000 jobs cut. Same week: record quarterly revenue.
- **Intuit**, May 20: over 3,000 employees laid off to "refocus on AI." FY26 guidance raised.
- **Cloudflare again** (via The Register's sidebar): stock sinks 18-24% after the announcement despite the beat.
- **Amazon**: 16,000 employees gone in January. AWS revenue growing.
- **Meta**: thousands of Bay Area layoffs in May. Q1 2026 earnings beat.

The tech industry has already cut more than 100,000 jobs this year, [per Statista](https://www.statista.com/chart/36198/tech-and-startup-employees-laid-off-worldwide/), on track to outpace both 2024 and 2025.

In almost every case, the framing is identical: *AI has made these roles unnecessary, we are not cutting costs, we are reinvesting in the future.* [Cloudflare's CEO email](https://blog.cloudflare.com/building-for-the-future/) to staff says explicitly: "Today's actions are not a cost-cutting exercise." GitLab's CEO says explicitly: "The cuts are not an AI optimization or cost cutting exercise."

Both companies then saved tens of millions in payroll.

---

## What's actually happening

There are three things converging here, and they're being bundled together under a single AI-flavored explanation.

**1. The 2022-2023 overhiring correction, still happening**

When interest rates were near zero, growth was the only mandate. Companies like GitLab, Cloudflare, and Atlassian hired aggressively because cheap capital rewarded headcount growth. Then rates went up, the "grow at all costs" era ended, and companies found themselves with organizational structures built for hypergrowth they no longer had. The correction has been slow because admitting you overhired is embarrassing. Blaming AI is not.

Simon Willison [noted on Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48100500) that GitLab's stock was around $52 a year ago and is $26 today — down 50% in 12 months. His read: "It's quite possible this is because they weren't making enough noise about their AI strategy." In other words, some of this is investor narrative management. The layoffs signal AI seriousness to a market that punishes companies who look like they're not pivoting fast enough.

**2. Growth deceleration, not collapse**

GitLab's FY27 revenue guidance implies 15-17% growth, down from 26% in FY26. That's still good. But deceleration from 26% to 16% in a market where GitHub Copilot holds 37% share and Cursor is eating developer mindshare is a real strategic problem. The restructuring into "60 smaller, more empowered R&D teams" and the country footprint reduction are a real response to that — but they're about speed and competitive repositioning, not AI-driven productivity gains making people redundant.

**3. Actual AI productivity gains — but not at the scale being claimed**

Cloudflare's internal AI usage growing 600% in three months sounds dramatic. But starting from near-zero and reaching "employees run thousands of AI agent sessions each day" is a long way from "we need 20% fewer people." The actual productivity math almost certainly doesn't support the headline number. What AI probably did is give executives a language to explain structural decisions they'd already made for other reasons.

The Hacker News commenter [put it sharply](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47172119): "I'm convinced that these 'AI Layoffs' are these companies trying to save face from the absurd overhiring that they did in 2022 and 2023 because apparently they thought that these no-interest loans/free money would just last forever. Now they can use AI as a scapegoat and people won't act like they were idiots for hiring twice as many people as they needed."

That's cynical, but the math supports it. If AI had genuinely automated 20% of Cloudflare's work in three months, you'd expect to see it in per-employee revenue metrics going back at least a year. Instead you see a company that's been growing fast and losing money while doing it, now deciding to restructure.

---

## Why the framing matters

This isn't just pedantic. The "AI made these jobs obsolete" frame has real consequences:

**It shapes policy responses.** The White House signed an AI executive order just yesterday, [dated June 2, 2026](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/promoting-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/), focused on innovation and cybersecurity. The political discourse around AI's labor impact is being shaped by corporate press releases that use "AI" as a universal solvent for every restructuring decision. If the real cause is overhiring correction and competitive repositioning, the policy responses to "AI is eliminating jobs" are solving the wrong problem.

**It obscures what companies actually owe workers.** "We didn't need you anymore because AI" is a different social contract than "we grew too fast and now we're right-sizing." The latter implies institutional failure and some responsibility. The former sounds like a force of nature.

**It overstates AI's current capabilities.** Every company that cuts 20% of staff and attributes it to AI is contributing to an inflated perception of what these tools can actually do today. That inflated perception drives hiring and investment decisions at other companies, which drives more layoffs when the expected productivity gains don't materialize at the claimed scale.

---

## What's real in GitLab's case

To be fair to Staples: GitLab's restructuring has some genuine strategic logic. Splitting R&D into 60 smaller teams is a real organizational bet that small, autonomous teams ship faster — which is true, and which is increasingly necessary when GitHub Copilot has Copilot Workspace and Cursor is running agentic code changes in background agents. GitLab needs to ship faster. That requires fewer coordination layers.

Exiting 22 countries where they have small teams and will "continue serving customers through partner networks" is also rational. Managing a handful of employees in 60+ countries is expensive in legal overhead and management attention. That has nothing to do with AI.

The AI agents story is real too — just smaller than advertised. Using LLMs to automate code review triage, PR summarization, and internal approval workflows probably does reduce the need for some coordination roles. But "reduces the need for some coordination roles" is a long way from "1,100 people made obsolete in three months."

---

## The number that matters

The Next Web [put it well](https://thenextweb.com/news/gitlab-layoffs-agentic-era-devops-ai): "Staples wrote that 'in some cases AI can augment and accelerate what team members have been doing, in other places we need to expand certain roles to go faster.' The sentence contains both a euphemism for job elimination and a promise of job creation. The ratio between the two is the number that matters, and it has not been disclosed."

That ratio — how many roles AI actually replaced versus how many the company is reinvesting in versus how many just disappeared because the company grew too fast — is the real story. It hasn't been disclosed by GitLab, Cloudflare, Cisco, or anyone else. Until it is, "AI made us do it" is a press release, not an explanation.

The 350 people leaving GitLab today deserved a cleaner answer than that.
